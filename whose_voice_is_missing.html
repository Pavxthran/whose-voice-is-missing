Why this skill matters

In many workplace teams, quieter voices can be overlooked in group discussions, especially where seniority, language confidence or unfamiliarity with the group affects participation. This module focuses on a specific and actionable skill: using inclusive communication to actively invite overlooked perspectives while reducing the dominance of those who naturally take up more space in a conversation.

Key concepts

Psychological safety

The shared belief that a team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking, including speaking up, disagreeing, or offering an idea that might be wrong.

Intersectionality

The way multiple aspects of identity and circumstance combine to create compounding advantages or barriers. Being junior, new, and a non-native speaker multiplies barriers beyond any single factor.

Inclusive facilitation

Structured approaches to meetings and discussions that create equal opportunity for contribution regardless of personality, culture, or seniority.

SMEAC

A structured briefing format: Situation, Mission, Execution, Admin and Logistics, Command and Signals. Used here to present realistic and complete workplace scenarios.

Facilitator notes: running this in 10-15 minutes

2 min

Introduce the module. Explain the SMEAC format and the learning outcome. Ask participants to work individually or in pairs.

8-10 min

Participants complete the five scenarios and the written practice exercise independently. Encourage them to say the practice phrases aloud after each feedback screen.

3 min

Group debrief using the three questions on the results screen. Ask which scenario felt most realistic and which strategy participants would apply in their own team.

Key takeaways

Exclusion in team discussions is usually unconscious, not intentional. Developing the habit of noticing who has spoken and who has not is the foundation of inclusive facilitation.
In-the-moment intervention is more effective than post-meeting repair. Redirecting back to an overlooked speaker restores their voice when it matters most and models inclusive norms for the whole group.
Intersectionality shapes who feels safe to speak. A person who is junior, new, and navigating a second language faces compounding barriers that are invisible to teammates who do not share those experiences.
Structural strategies such as direct named invitation and structured round-robins remove the burden of self-advocacy and create a norm where every voice is expected, not just permitted.

In your next team meeting

Try this in your next team meeting
Notice who has not spoken and make a point of inviting them by name
Invite input directly and respectfully, without putting anyone on the spot
Return to speakers who were interrupted and give them back the floor
Use a structured round before opening discussion to equalise participation
Do not place the burden of inclusion on quieter members; make it a facilitation responsibility
